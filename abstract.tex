\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent retrieval systems that let agents' scoring parameters drift via
inter-agent communication risk a silent failure mode: a consensus update can
reorder a query's top-$k$ retrieved documents even when no single agent's
parameters change by much. We introduce a predictive, pre-communication
certificate for top-$k$ stability of a bilinear retrieval head
$s_W(q,d) = (Wq)^\top(Wd)$ under one deterministic consensus step
$W \to W + \Delta W$. Using the operator norm's submultiplicativity, each
candidate document $i$ admits a per-document score-change bound
$B_i = \|q\|\,\|d_i\|\,[2\|W\|_2\|\Delta W\|_2 + \|\Delta W\|_2^2]$, computed
entirely from $W$ and the \emph{proposed} $\Delta W$ before any rescoring. We
report three certificates built from the same bounds: a global form,
$m_k > 2B_{\max}$; a strictly tighter pairwise form, certifying whenever the
slack $C = \min_{i\in T_k,\,j\notin T_k}[s_i - s_j - B_i - B_j] > 0$; and an
ordered form that additionally checks each adjacent-rank gap
$s_{(r)} - s_{(r+1)} > B_{(r)} + B_{(r+1)}$ for $r = 1,\dots,k-1$ to certify
the exact ranking, not just the set, is unchanged. The global form implies
the pairwise form, and the ordered form is the pairwise form plus those
adjacent-rank conditions; the pairwise form does not imply the ordered one,
since a tie inside the top-$k$ is certified for membership and rejected for
ordering. We use the pairwise form as the primary certificate. We
validate all three on three multi-hop QA datasets (HotpotQA,
MuSiQue, 2WikiMultihopQA; $\approx 989$ queries each) crossed with three
pretrained retrievers (MiniLM, E5, BGE), each respecting its own
query/passage formatting convention, over 5 diffusion hops and consensus
blend rates $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8\}$, with no injected noise,
the regime the certificates' proofs cover. Across all $3\times3\times25 =
225$ dataset$\times$retriever$\times$hop$\times\alpha$ configurations and a
separate candidate-pool-size ablation (10/20/50 candidates, all three
datasets), every certificate reports zero soundness violations: whenever a
query is certified stable, its top-$k$ is empirically unchanged, in every
case. The certificates are non-trivial rather than vacuous: the pairwise
rate is $0\%$ at low $\alpha$ and rises to $35$–$60\%$ of queries by the
final hop at $\alpha=0.8$ for MiniLM and BGE (E5 stays comparatively low,
$\approx 12$–$15\%$, showing the effect is retriever-dependent), tracking
the shrinking per-hop parameter disagreement as agents approach consensus,
while NDCG@5 ($0.49$–$0.87$ across cells) stays meaningful and stable
throughout every dataset and retriever, confirming the ranking being
certified is real rather than an artifact of untrained heads. Certification
rate falls as the candidate pool grows (more pairs to simultaneously
satisfy), a further sign the guarantee is substantive rather than vacuous.
Gaussian noise added on top of the deterministic step is outside the
certificates' guarantee and can violate it, so the certificate experiments
reject any nonzero noise rather than reporting certified rates under it.
\end{abstract}
