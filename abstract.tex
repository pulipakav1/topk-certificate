\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent retrieval systems that route information through similarity graphs
typically weight edges by embedding-space proximity, ignoring the geometry of
the underlying consensus process itself. We introduce Spectral Information
Routing (SIR), which weights the inter-agent communication graph using
Fisher--Rao geodesic distances between agents' local likelihood geometries
rather than cosine similarity, and establish a spectral bound (Theorem~1)
relating post-diffusion non-consensus representation variance to the graph's
algebraic connectivity ($\lambda_2$) and the per-hop Fisher-information gap.
We validate this bound on the full HotpotQA distractor validation set (989
multi-hop QA queries, 5 diffusion hops) against a cosine-similarity
Graph-of-Thoughts baseline under matched edge budgets. SIR reliably increases
algebraic connectivity and reduces cumulative Fisher-manifold drift relative
to baseline, and routes roughly 5\% more probability mass to gold-evidence
agents. However, this structural improvement does not translate into better
discrete retrieval ranking: SIR consistently underperforms baseline on
NDCG@5 and F1@5 (0.3403 vs.\ 0.3516 and 0.2666 vs.\ 0.2817, respectively),
and per-query $z$-standardized score-margin analysis shows the gap is a
static offset present from the first hop rather than a cumulative, hop-wise
degradation. Theorem~1's bound holds with wide slack at every hop for both
strategies. Together these results substantiate the spectral bound while
exposing a structural misalignment between continuous Fisher-manifold
consensus objectives and discrete rank-ordered retrieval objectives -- a gap
hyperparameter tuning alone cannot close.
\end{abstract}
